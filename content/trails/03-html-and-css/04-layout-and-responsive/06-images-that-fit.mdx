<Analogy title="A poster and a frame">
If you try to hang a huge poster in a small frame, it sticks out over the edges. You need to **shrink it to fit**, and shrink it evenly, or the mountains end up looking squashed.
</Analogy>

Photos are often much bigger than the space they're shown in. On a phone, a big photo can stick out past the edge of the screen and make the whole page scroll sideways (Stack Quest's own tests check for exactly that problem).

Professionals add this rule to almost every stylesheet:

```css
img {
  max-width: 100%;
  height: auto;
}
```

- `max-width: 100%` means "be your natural size, but **never wider than your container**".
- `height: auto` means "work out the height from the width", so the picture keeps its shape instead of being squashed.

## Fix the bug

The hike photo is forced to be 1200px wide, so it spills right out of its 300px card. Make it fit the card **and** keep its shape.
