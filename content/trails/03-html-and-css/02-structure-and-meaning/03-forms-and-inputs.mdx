<Analogy title="A paper form at the clinic">
At a clinic you fill in a paper form: each box has a printed **label** next to it ("Name", "ID number", "Date of birth"), and you hand it in at the counter when you're done. A web <Term word="form">form</Term> is the same: labels, boxes to fill in, and a button to hand it in.
</Analogy>

## The pieces of a form

```html
<form>
  <label for="phone">Cellphone number</label>
  <input id="phone" type="tel">
  <button type="submit">Send</button>
</form>
```

- `form` wraps everything that gets sent together.
- `input` is a box to fill in. It has no closing tag.
- `label` is the printed words next to the box. Its `for` attribute must match the input's `id`. That **links** them: tapping the label puts the cursor in the box, and a <Term word="screen-reader">screen reader</Term> reads the label aloud when you reach the box. Never leave an input without a label.
- `button type="submit"` hands the form in.

## Choose the right type

The `type` attribute changes how an input behaves, especially on phones:

- `type="text"`: any text (the default).
- `type="email"`: the phone keyboard shows **@**, and the browser checks it looks like an email address.
- `type="tel"`: shows a number pad for phone numbers.
- `type="password"`: hides what you type behind dots.
- `type="number"`: only numbers.

Add `required` to an input and the browser won't let the form be sent while it's empty.

For longer text, like a message, use `textarea` instead. Unlike `input`, it has a closing tag: `<textarea id="msg"></textarea>`.

The book club form has a name field. Add:

1. A **label** that says "Your email"
2. An **email input** that's **required**, linked to the label
3. A **submit button** that says "Join"

The practice preview is sealed, so its forms never send anything anywhere, and it won't show the "please fill in this field" message. On a real website, pressing the button with the email box empty would stop you with that message.

<KeyIdea>
Every `input` needs a `label` whose `for` matches the input's `id`. Pick the `type` that fits the information, and add `required` when it can't be left empty.
</KeyIdea>
