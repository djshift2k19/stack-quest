<Analogy title="Signs in a shopping mall">
A mall puts up signs: "Entrance", "Food court", "Toilets", "Exit". You could find your way without them, eventually. But the signs let *everyone* find things quickly, including someone who's visiting for the first time.
</Analogy>

## Boxes with names

There's an HTML element called `div` (short for "division"). It's a plain box that means nothing. For years, people built whole pages out of `div`s:

```html
<div class="top">...</div>
<div class="menu">...</div>
<div class="content">...</div>
```

That works, but the browser has no idea what each box is *for*. Modern HTML gives you boxes with **names** that say what they mean. This is called <Term word="semantic-html">semantic HTML</Term> ("semantic" means "to do with meaning"):

- `header`: the top part, usually the site name or logo.
- `nav`: the main menu of links (navigation).
- `main`: the main content of this page. Use only **one**.
- `section`: a group of related content, normally with its own heading.
- `article`: a piece that makes sense on its own, like a news story or a blog post.
- `footer`: the bottom part, with contact details, copyright and so on.

```html
<body>
  <header>
    <h1>Mama Joy's Kitchen</h1>
    <nav>
      <a href="/menu">Menu</a>
      <a href="/contact">Contact</a>
    </nav>
  </header>
  <main>
    <h2>Today's special</h2>
    <p>Chicken curry with rice.</p>
  </main>
  <footer>
    <p>Open Monday to Saturday, 8am to 5pm.</p>
  </footer>
</body>
```

## Why bother, if it looks the same?

On screen, a `header` looks exactly like a `div`. The difference is for:

1. **People using a <Term word="screen-reader">screen reader</Term>.** They can jump straight to the `main` content or the `nav`, instead of listening to the whole page from the top.
2. **Search engines**, which understand what your page is about.
3. **Other developers**, including you in six months. `footer` explains itself; `div class="box3"` doesn't.

<KeyIdea>
Choose elements for what the content **means**, not how it looks: `header`, `nav`, `main`, `section`, `article` and `footer`. Use a plain `div` only when nothing else fits.
</KeyIdea>
