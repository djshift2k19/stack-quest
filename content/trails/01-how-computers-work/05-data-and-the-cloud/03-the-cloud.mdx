<Analogy title="Generator versus Eskom">
You could buy and run your own generator: you pay upfront, maintain it, buy fuel and fix it when it breaks. Or you plug into the grid and pay for what you use. <Term word="cloud">The cloud</Term> is plugging into a "grid" of computers instead of owning your own.
</Analogy>

## It's just someone else's computers

"The cloud" isn't in the sky. It's servers in <Term word="data-centre">data centres</Term> owned by companies like Amazon (AWS), Microsoft (Azure), Google, Vercel and Supabase. You rent them over the internet. Amazon, Microsoft and Google all have data centres in South Africa, which makes things faster for local users.

## What you can rent

- **Storage**: keeping files, like Google Photos or iCloud backups.
- **Servers**: running your back end.
- **Databases**: run and backed up for you.
- **Hosting**: putting your website online. Stack Quest will use Vercel's free plan in Phase 4.

## The trade-offs

**Good:**

- No machines to buy or fix.
- Start free or very cheap, and grow when you need to.
- Backups and security are handled by experts.

**Watch out for:**

- **Cost surprises.** You pay for usage, and a mistake (like an endless loop calling a paid service) can run up a bill. Always set spending limits and alerts. Trail 10 covers this.
- **Data location and privacy.** In South Africa, **POPIA** (the Protection of Personal Information Act) says how personal data must be handled, wherever it's stored. Trail 8 covers this.
- **Dependence.** If the provider has an outage, so do you.

<KeyIdea>
**The cloud** is renting someone else's computers over the internet. It's convenient and starts cheap, but always watch **costs** and **where personal data goes**.
</KeyIdea>
