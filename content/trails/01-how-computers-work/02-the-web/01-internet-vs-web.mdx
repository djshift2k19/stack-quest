<Analogy title="Roads and taxis">
South Africa's roads connect every town. Many different things travel on them: minibus taxis, delivery trucks, ambulances. The roads aren't the taxis, and the taxis aren't the roads.
</Analogy>

## The internet: the roads

The <Term word="internet">internet</Term> is a worldwide network of connected computers. They're linked by undersea cables (several land at Mtunzini, Yzerfontein and Melkbosstrand), fibre in the ground, cell towers and satellites. Its only job is to carry data from one computer to another.

## The web: one kind of traffic

<Term word="web">The web</Term> is a huge collection of pages you visit with a browser: news sites, Wikipedia, online shops. It's one kind of traffic travelling on the internet. There are others:

- **WhatsApp messages** travel over the internet, but they aren't the web.
- **Email** travels over the internet, but it isn't the web either.
- **Netflix streaming** uses the internet, and mostly not web pages.

So when your data runs out, it's your **internet** access that stops. Every kind of traffic stops with it, the web included.

## Pages and sites

- A **web page** is one document, such as one news article.
- A <Term word="website">website</Term> is a collection of related pages under one name, like a book made of pages.

<KeyIdea>
The **internet** is the network: the roads. The **web** is the pages you visit in a browser: one kind of traffic on those roads. As a full-stack developer, you'll build things for the web that travel over the internet.
</KeyIdea>
