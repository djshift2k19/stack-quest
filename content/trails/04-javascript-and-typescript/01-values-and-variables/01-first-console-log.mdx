<Analogy title="Talking to yourself out loud">
When you work something out in your head, it helps to say steps out loud: "OK, 3 bags at R20, that's R60." `console.log` is how a program says things out loud, so **you** can hear what it's thinking.
</Analogy>

In Trail 3 you gave pages **structure** (HTML) and **looks** (CSS). Now for the third layer: <Term word="javascript">JavaScript</Term>, which makes things **happen**.

Here's your first line of JavaScript:

```javascript
console.log("Sawubona, world!");
```

- `console.log(...)` prints whatever is inside the round brackets to the <Term word="console">console</Term>, a text area for messages. Visitors to a website never see it; it's for developers.
- `"Sawubona, world!"` is a piece of text. Text in code always goes **inside quote marks**, so JavaScript knows it's words to print and not instructions to follow.
- The `;` at the end finishes the instruction, like a full stop at the end of a sentence.

Type it exactly and press **Check**. Then press **▶ Run what you typed** to see it printed.

Every developer, in every language, starts with a "hello, world" program. You're in good company.

<KeyIdea>
`console.log(...)` prints a message to the console. Text goes inside **quote marks**, and each instruction ends with `;`.
</KeyIdea>
