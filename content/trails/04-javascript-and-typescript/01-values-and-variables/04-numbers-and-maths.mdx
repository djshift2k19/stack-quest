<Term word="number">Numbers</Term> in JavaScript work like a calculator. These symbols are called <Term word="operator">operators</Term>:

- `+` adds, `-` subtracts
- `*` multiplies (not `×`)
- `/` divides (not `÷`)
- `%` gives the **remainder** after dividing: `7 % 2` is `1`. It's handy for "is this number even?"

Just like in maths class, `*` and `/` happen before `+` and `-`, and **brackets go first**. So `2 + 3 * 4` is `14`, but `(2 + 3) * 4` is `20`.

Numbers are written **without** quote marks, and without spaces or commas: `1500`, not `"1500"` or `1 500`. Decimals use a full stop: `0.15`.
