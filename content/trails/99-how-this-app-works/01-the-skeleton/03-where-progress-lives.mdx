<Analogy title="A notebook in your bag">
Some apps keep your progress in a notebook at head office (a server far away). Stack Quest, for now, keeps it in a notebook **in your own bag**: your browser. It's always with you, it works without signal, and nobody else can read it. But if you throw the bag away, the notebook goes with it.
</Analogy>

## IndexedDB: a small database in your browser

Every modern browser has a built-in <Term word="database">database</Term> called **IndexedDB**. A database is simply an organised place to store information so you can find it again later.

When you finish a unit, Stack Quest writes a record like this into IndexedDB:

```json
{
  "xp": 10,
  "units": {
    "how-computers-work/your-computer/what-is-a-computer": {
      "completions": 1,
      "fewestHints": 0,
      "xpEarned": 10
    }
  },
  "streak": { "current": 1, "longest": 1, "freezes": 1 }
}
```

The code that does the saving is in `src/lib/progress/storage.ts`. It's short. Have a look!

## Why not use an account?

Accounts need a server, sign-up forms and passwords, which is a lot to build and a lot to ask of you on day one. Keeping progress in the browser means:

- **No sign-up**: open the app and start.
- **Works offline**: nothing has to travel over the internet.
- **Private**: your data stays on your device.

The trade-off: progress lives in **this browser on this device**. Use a different browser, or clear your "site data", and it starts fresh. In Phase 4 you'll add optional accounts so progress can sync between your Mac and your phone.

## Peek at it yourself

In Chrome: open the menu **View → Developer → Developer Tools**, click the **Application** tab, then **IndexedDB → stack-quest → kv**. You're looking at the actual notebook.

<KeyIdea>
Your progress is stored in **IndexedDB**, a database inside your browser. It's fast, private and works offline, but it lives in one browser on one device until accounts arrive in Phase 4.
</KeyIdea>
