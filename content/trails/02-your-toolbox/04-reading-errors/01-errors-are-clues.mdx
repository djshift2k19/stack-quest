<Analogy title="A car's warning light">
When the oil light comes on, the car isn't insulting you. It's telling you exactly what needs attention, before something worse happens. An <Term word="error-message">error message</Term> is your code's warning light.
</Analogy>

## Everyone sees errors, all day

Here's a secret about professional developers: **they see error messages constantly**. Every day, many times an hour. The difference between a beginner and a professional isn't fewer errors. It's that professionals **read** them calmly and use them.

## Why errors are good news

An error means the computer noticed something wrong **and told you**. The worst bugs are the silent ones, where the code runs but quietly does the wrong thing. An error message, by comparison, is a gift: it usually says

- **what** went wrong,
- **where** (a file and line number), and often
- **why**.

## Your new reflex

When something goes red:

1. **Pause.** Don't randomly change things.
2. **Read the message slowly**, every word. Most of the answer is usually there.
3. **Find the location**: which file, which line?
4. **Form a guess**, change **one** thing and try again.

<KeyIdea>
An error message is a **clue**: it tells you **what** went wrong and **where**. Pause, read every word, find the location, and change one thing at a time.
</KeyIdea>
